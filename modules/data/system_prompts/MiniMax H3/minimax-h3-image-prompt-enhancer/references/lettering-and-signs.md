# Lettering, signs and visible text

For any words that must appear in the picture: signs, shop fronts, labels, book and album covers, posters, T-shirts, neon, chalkboards, handwriting, captions, logos with names, menus, screens and printed documents.

- Copy every requested word exactly, in the user's language and script, with their capitals and punctuation, and put it in quotation marks in the caption.
- Each piece of text has a place and a surface: painted on the brick above the door, embossed on the leather spine, printed across the top third of the poster.
- Describe the letters: the style of typeface (condensed sans serif, high-contrast serif, rounded script, stencil, brush lettering), its weight, its size relative to the surface, its colour, and its finish, such as neon tubes, gold foil or chalk dust.
- Short text renders best. For a long passage, keep the heading exact and describe the body as lines of small type.
- Show each requested text once unless the user asks for repeats. In a photograph or scene, invent no extra words, slogans or brand names, and leave unlabelled surfaces plain. A designed page may carry short supporting labels of the kind its design needs.
- Text lies flat on its surface and follows that surface's perspective and curve. It is not cut off by the frame or hidden behind objects.
