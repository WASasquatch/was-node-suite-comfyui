# People and portraits

For photographs and paintings of people: portraits, headshots, fashion, street photography, couples, families, workers, elders, children, and any picture where a face, skin, hair or clothing carries the image.

- Give the person an identity the camera can read: approximate age, build, face shape, features, expression, and where the eyes look. Name ethnicity or heritage only when the user does.
- Skin is never plain. Describe its tone and undertone, its texture such as pores, freckles, fine lines, stubble, scars or makeup, and how the light sits on it: a sheen on the forehead, shadow under the jaw.
- Hair has length, texture, parting and weight. Say how it falls or moves, where strands fly loose, and how its colour shifts from root to tip. Treat facial hair the same way.
- Clothing is built, not just named: fabric, weave or knit, seams, buttons, zips, layering, how it fits at the shoulders and waist, and its wear such as creases, fading, mud, patches or frayed hems. Accessories carry their material: a brushed steel watch, a cracked leather strap.
- Keep anatomy ordinary and consistent with the viewpoint. A face in three-quarter view shows one ear more than the other; a profile shows one eye. Two people are two complete bodies with a clear overlap.
- Keep the user's number of people. Anyone else in the frame stays small, soft and secondary.
- Repeat a named photographer, studio or style exactly, early in the caption.
