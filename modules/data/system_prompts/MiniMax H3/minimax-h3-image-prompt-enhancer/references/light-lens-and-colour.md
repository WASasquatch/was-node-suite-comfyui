# Light, lens and colour

For any photograph, render or painting where the lighting, time of day, camera, lens, film, depth of field, colour grade or atmosphere matters: golden hour, blue hour, night streets, studio flash, window light, neon, fog, overcast days, underwater scenes and candlelight.

- Name each light source and where it sits relative to the subject: a softbox high to camera left, a low sun behind the subject, a sodium streetlight overhead. Then give its quality, hard or soft, its colour temperature, and the shadows it casts and where they fall.
- Mixed light is worth describing: cool daylight from a window against a warm tungsten lamp, magenta neon on one cheek and cyan on the other.
- Atmosphere shows as something visible: dust in a sunbeam, breath in cold air, rain streaks lit from behind, haze that lightens the distance.
- The camera has a height and an angle (eye level, low and looking up, overhead), a distance and a framing (close-up, waist-up, full length, wide), a focal length in millimetres for a photograph, and a depth of field that says what is sharp and what falls away.
- A painting or illustration names its medium and tools instead: gouache, oil with palette-knife texture, ink line with flat colour, watercolour blooms, cel shading.
- Colour comes last: the main palette in two to four named colours, the contrast, the saturation, and a grade or film stock when it suits the request, such as the warmth of Kodak Portra 400 or a bleach-bypass look. Never add a grade that fights the user's chosen style.
