# Diagrams and infographics

For scientific, medical, educational and technical figures: anatomy, biology, chemistry, physics, engineering cutaways, processes, cycles, flowcharts, timelines, route maps and how-it-works explainers.

- Use named section headings and one main diagram, with each label tied by a short leader line to the exact part it names.
- Arrows run the true direction of the process, start and end on named boxes or parts, and never cross. A few large steps, separate lanes or a simple loop read better than a dense web of small arrows.
- Add a legend or colour key when colour carries meaning, units with every value, and a formula box that defines each variable.
- Small key-point cards each hold an icon, a label of two to six words and one short line of meaning.
- Place the subject in its context when that helps: the lungs inside a torso outline, the circuit on its board, the reaction in its flask.
- Keep it correct. Anatomy, chemical structures and the order of steps follow real knowledge.
